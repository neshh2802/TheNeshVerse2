Learning by making .

October 2026 · 3 min read

A note on why making something of your own can change the way you learn.

There is a difference between knowing a concept and reaching for it when a real problem shows up. I notice that difference most when I try to make something.

A project has a way of turning vague curiosity into better questions. You discover the part you do not understand because your idea cannot move forward without it. Then learning has somewhere to go.

That does not make every experiment successful, and it does not mean every subject needs to become an app. It just means that sometimes a small, imperfect thing you make can teach you what a perfect set of notes could not.

I want to keep that loop close: learn a little, try it, notice what I missed, and begin again.
